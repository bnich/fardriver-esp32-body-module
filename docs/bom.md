## JLC assembly — the custom boards

The custom boards are **4-layer** (JLC's standard 1.6 mm stack) and assembled by JLC from **LCSC
parts, JLC Basic first**. The LCSC part does not have to be the part bought for the breadboard
above. Every LCSC number lives in [`tools/netlist.py`](../tools/netlist.py), and nowhere else:
- parts bought by value: `_R_LCSC` and `_C_LCSC`;
- parts bought by part number: `_FAB_BY_MPN`, and `_LOOSE_BY_MPN` for the loose parts below;
- the harness terminals' headers and plugs: `_TERMINALS`; other connectors: `_FAB_CONN`.

A resistor's or capacitor's working voltage is its chosen part's, and the rules check it against the
circuit. `python3 -m tools.jlc_bom --out bom.csv` writes the file JLC's order takes. Run without
`--out`, it also lists what is ordered loose and what is hand-soldered, with every LCSC code and
count — order from that list, not from this page.

**Ordered loose with the boards, fitted by you.** JLC inserts through-hole parts upright; the
height budget counts these lying down, and the fuse clips in.

## Notes

- **Reasoning lives in [`plan.md`](plan.md)**, not here — §3.2 power/D13 · §4 interface classes
  and the wetting-current rule · §6 outputs · §9.2 the three boards and the rows · §9.5 sourcing ·
  §8 the decision table. ⚠️ **Change a part here first**, then the plan section that carries its reasoning.
- ⬜ **Not yet checked against what is already on the shelf** — the BOM was built from what the docs
  specify. Likely candidates: A2 socket strips · D7 screw terminals · the generic passives (A7, B3, C1,
  D2, D9, D12, D13, D14, E14, E19, E21, G4). A shelf pass cuts both cost and price-hunting.
- ⚠️ **D1 is the hardest hand mount on the BOM.** `TPS4H160BQPWPRQ1` is **28-HTSSOP, 0.65 mm pitch,
  with an exposed thermal pad** carrying the heat of its four current-limited channels, and **the pad
  must reach real copper or the per-channel current limiting and thermal shutdown will not behave as
  specified.** On the board set JLC places all three, and the layout ties each pad to ground copper. For the
  breadboard, solder a spare to an X7 adapter first and prove the technique. A5's PowerPAD is the
  same soldering class. *(Plan §9.1 covers what may and may not sit on a breadboard.)*
- **Specs come from the manufacturer PDF (`pdftotext`), never a web-fetch summary** — the summary got C2's
  polarity and pitch wrong (plan §12).
- ⛔ **A TVS follows its line's IDLE voltage, not the logic level behind it.** C2 (5 V) on a 12 V
  line is a dead short that nothing in a schematic shows; C4 (15 V) on the raw
  12 V rail sits inside the TDK's over-voltage window. Four jobs: **C2 on every ≤5 V line, the brake
  levers included** (they are 3.3 V nodes since plan D23) · C4 on `BL` / `ACC+` and the boost
  output · D10 the 12 V lamp, load and aux outputs (`SMF18A`) and the rail itself (`SMBJ18A`) ·
  **D15 (`SMF6.0A`) the four 5 V aux outputs** (plan §6.2.4). Every TVS in the netlist states its clamping voltage, and
  the rules hold each part it protects to it.
- ⚠️ **Harness terminals come in four families, by job, and the rows follow them** — 7.62 mm (X11)
  pack voltage only · 5.08 mm (X9) the FarDriver leads only · **3.50 mm (X12) the 5 V outputs only** ·
  3.81 mm (X8) the 12 V row and the INPUTS row — so a plug of one dangerous job cannot seat in a
  header of another. Within the shared 3.81 mm family a smaller plug seats, offset, in a larger
  header, so **no 12 V terminal shares a size with an input terminal**: with every plug seated, a
  mis-plug leaves a plug in the hand. One plug
  per harness bundle, nothing to crimp, no housing to match: wire it outside the box, push it in from
  the board edge, and two screws lock it to the header's flanges.
- ⚠️ **Meter the boost wire before connecting a 30 V FET to it.** §7.1's *"3.3–12 V pull-up inside the
  controller"* is unsourced, and the candidate wire (`CruisePin` PIN17) has never been metered.
  `BOOST_OUT` carries C4's 15 V array (V<sub>BR</sub> 16.7 V min), so **the pull-up must measure
  ≤ 15 V** for the `AO3400A` path; above that, the array would conduct continuously — use the
  **PC817** output plan §6 offers for the boost line instead. ⛔ **The same 30-pin harness carries
  pink `60VC` at 72–84 V** — landing on it destroys any low-voltage FET, so this is a measurement,
  not a part choice. Keep `60VC` capped and taped back on its own (pull its pin from the plug if
  possible): shorted onto a serial wire, it burns `R436` / `R443`. Procedure:
  [`inputs-bench-session.md`](inputs-bench-session.md) §7, C1.5.
- ⛔ **Fuse and holder part numbers look interchangeable and are not.** For anything fuse-related, read
  the datasheet's **fuse-type line, the dimension drawing and the ordering-table footnotes** before the
  price. Four that looked right: Schurter **`FST`** (same 5×20 size, glass, **no DC rating**) · Schurter
  **`SP`** (ceramic, fast-acting, high-breaking — **no DC rating anywhere**) · Littelfuse
  **`01500330HXU`** (looked like E3's holder; actually **6.3×32 mm 3AG**, **IP40**, *"certifications do
  not apply"*) · Schurter **`FAC 0031.3803`** (E6 — electrically right, **47.5 mm tall**, sold without
  its cap).
- ⚠️ **Tariffs are in no figure here.** Mouser added **$31.97 (25%)** on E1's $127.88, and DigiKey order
  101547984 added **$26.09**. Treat every all-in number as **ex-duty**.
- **Holders and elements are one purchase:** E3 + E4 (inline, B+ tap) and E11 + E22 (clips on POWER,
  DC-DC #2). Neither works half-bought — including when reordering spares.
- ⚠️ **E3 has no spares and no substitute.** A 3 A fast-blow at **600 VDC / 50 kA DC** is a narrow part —
  Schurter has **no** fast-acting 5×20 with any DC rating. A bring-up wiring error will pop it, a fault
  behind E11 can take it too (melting I²t 2.4 A²s against the 1 A T-lag's 1.1 A²s — too close to

- ⬜ **Keep the TDK CN-B datasheet and instruction manual with the other vendor documents** under
